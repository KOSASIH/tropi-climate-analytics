Air quality monitoring at national scale — how we do it.

Indonesia's official air quality standard (ISPU, per PERMENLHK P.14/2020) relies on 147 KLHK ground monitoring stations. Good coverage in Java and Sumatra — but significant gaps in Kalimantan, Sulawesi, and Papua.

Tropi fills those gaps by fusing MODIS MAIAC satellite aerosol retrievals with KLHK's ground network, producing bias-corrected PM2.5 estimates for all 514 districts hourly.

The result: Indonesia's first truly national, real-time air quality map.

Most critical during fire season (July–October): when peat fires release smoke across Kalimantan and Sumatera, satellite-derived data is the only way to track exposure across remote populations.

Data access: https://docs.tropi-climate-analytics.id/air-quality

#TropiClimate #AirQuality #RemoteSensing #PublicHealth #IndonesiaClimate

How do you monitor deforestation during Indonesia's wet season?

With optical satellites like Landsat, you can't — persistent cloud cover obscures up to 80% of Kalimantan and Sumatera between October and April. That's the same period when illegal clearing is most active.

Our answer: SAR-optical fusion.

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), provided through our LAPAN/BRIN partnership, penetrates cloud and rain cover entirely. Combined with Landsat optical imagery during clear periods, we build continuous deforestation maps year-round.

The result: 20× improvement in effective monitoring coverage during the wet season. Monthly SAR-optical fusion deforestation products are now available for Kalimantan and Sumatera.

Technical details → https://docs.tropi-climate-analytics.id/deforestation
